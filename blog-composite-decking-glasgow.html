Composite Decking Cost in Glasgow: 2026 Prices & Installation Guide

3rd August 2026 Brickscapes Team, Glasgow 8 min read

If you're planning a new garden deck in Glasgow, the first and most important question is almost always: how much is it going to cost?

In 2026, composite decking in Glasgow typically costs between £140 and £220 per m² fully installed, depending on board quality, subframe specification, ground conditions, and design complexity. For an average 20m² residential back garden deck, you are usually looking at £2,800 to £4,400 fully fitted.

If you're deciding between composite and traditional timber, the main trade-off is straightforward: timber costs less upfront, while composite costs more initially but requires considerably less maintenance to survive Glasgow's wet climate.

What Is Included in a Professional Glasgow Decking Quote?

When comparing decking quotes in Glasgow, always confirm that the quote is fully itemised. A complete, professional installation quote from Brickscapes includes:

  • Ground preparation: Excavation, soil leveling, and laying a commercial-grade geo-textile weed suppression membrane to prevent plant growth beneath the boards.
  • Substructure framing: Structural C24 pressure-treated timber joists (or composite joists) installed at 300mm–400mm centres for rigid support.
  • Foundations: Concrete footing pads, structural posts, or heavy-duty adjustable pedestals to keep the subframe off damp soil.
  • Composite boards: High-density capped composite boards in your chosen colour and profile.
  • Concealed fixings: Hidden clip fastener systems with stainless steel screws, leaving no visible screw heads or splinter hazards on top.
  • Edging & fascia trims: Colour-matched perimeter bullnose boards or skirting fascia to conceal subframe timbers and give a clean, finished look.
  • Waste removal: Full cleanup and disposal of all turf, excavated earth, and packaging.

What Makes Composite Decking More Expensive?

Every garden is different. If your quote is higher than the base £140/m² rate, it is usually driven by one or more of these real-world site factors:

  • Sloping or uneven ground: Many Glasgow gardens — particularly in Bearsden, Milngavie, the West End, and parts of the Southside — sit on noticeable slopes. Building over a slope requires taller structural support posts, extra timber bracing, and deeper concrete foundations.
  • Raised platforms, steps, and balustrades: A deck raised more than 300mm off the ground typically requires access steps and safety balustrades. Adding glass balustrade panels, powder-coated aluminium railings, or composite spindles adds both material and labour cost.
  • Restricted site access: In tenement properties across Shawlands, Pollokshields, or terraced homes with no rear lane, all materials and excavated spoil must be carried through closes or narrow hallways by hand, which increases labour hours.
  • Subframe material upgrades: Adding joist barrier tape (which protects timber joist tops from standing rainwater) or upgrading to a 100% aluminium/composite subframe adds an upfront cost but ensures the subframe lasts as long as the composite boards.
  • Integrated features: Built-in LED step lighting, recessed spotlights, integrated seating benches, flush planters, or concealed under-deck storage access doors.

Is Composite Cheaper Than Timber Over Time?

Treated softwood timber is visibly cheaper on day one. However, Glasgow receives over 1,200mm of rainfall per year — roughly twice as much as London. In our climate, maintaining a timber deck requires ongoing care: power washing, applying anti-fungal treatments, and coating with decking oil or stain.

If you pay a professional to clean and treat a 20m² timber deck every 1–2 years, it typically costs £150–£300 per treatment. If you do it yourself, it requires multiple dry days and quality materials. Over a 10 to 15-year period, the cumulative cost of stains, sealants, and replacing soft or rotted boards significantly narrows the price gap between timber and composite.

If you plan to stay in your property for many years and value having a deck that is always ready to use without annual sanding and staining weekends, composite provides substantial long-term convenience and reliable value.

Glasgow Climate & Specification Tips

  • Subframe ventilation is crucial: In wet climates like Glasgow, composite decks must maintain adequate ventilation beneath the boards (at least 50mm airflow gap). This allows moisture from Scottish rain to evaporate freely rather than getting trapped beneath the frame.
  • Use joist tape on timber subframes: Applying protective butyl joist tape along the top of all timber substructure joists prevents standing water from sitting in the screw points and prolongs subframe life.
  • Popular colour choices: Charcoal grey and dark anthracite remain the most requested colours across Glasgow — they pair cleanly with white render, grey window frames, and contemporary garden walls. Teak and warm oak tones remain popular for traditional sandstone properties in Bearsden and Giffnock.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does composite decking cost per m² in Glasgow?

In 2026, composite decking installation in Glasgow typically ranges from £140 to £220 per m² fully supplied and fitted. Entry-level hollow boards cost £140–£155/m², mid-range solid capped boards run £165–£190/m², and premium co-extruded boards cost £195–£220+/m².

How much does a typical garden deck cost in Glasgow?

A standard 20m² residential back garden deck in Glasgow usually costs between £2,800 and £4,400 fully installed. Smaller 10m² bistro decks cost around £1,400 to £2,200, while large 30m² multi-zone decks range from £4,200 to £6,600.

Is composite decking worth the extra cost over timber in Scotland?

For most homeowners, yes. Timber is cheaper upfront, but Glasgow's 1,200mm+ annual rainfall requires regular cleaning, staining, and sealing (£150–£300/yr or extensive DIY time) to prevent rot and algae. Composite requires only occasional soapy washing and lasts 25–30+ years.

Does composite decking get slippery in wet Scottish weather?

Quality composite decking with an embossed woodgrain or brushed texture provides excellent traction, even during heavy Scottish downpours. Unlike timber, composite does not absorb moisture or cultivate slippery green algae slime.

What colour composite decking is most popular in Glasgow?

Charcoal grey and dark anthracite are the most popular colours across Greater Glasgow, pairing well with modern bi-fold doors and render. Warm oak, spiced teak, and silver-grey are also popular for traditional gardens.
