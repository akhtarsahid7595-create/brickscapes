What Are the Structural Requirements for Facing Brick Retaining Walls in Scottish Clay Soils?

Direct Answer: Retaining brick walls built on West of Scotland clay soils require double-skin facing brickwork constructed over C25 steel-reinforced concrete trench footings (150mm–300mm deep) with sulphate-resistant M4 mortar, Hyload DPC membranes, and sub-surface weep vents every 1000mm to safely discharge hydrostatic groundwater pressure.

Heavy clay soils prevalent in Bearsden, Clydebank, and Newton Mearns expand significantly when saturated during Scottish winter rainfall. Without steel-reinforced trench foundations and weep vents, unreinforced masonry walls experience lateral shear failure within 3 to 7 years.
